About us

About CLIFF

The Cambridge Lucy India Futures Forum is an initiative of Lucy Cavendish College, University of Cambridge, launched on 26 June 2026.

Mission and vision

Lucy Cavendish exists to attract, support and empower students who want to lead change. Its Strategy 2030 asks the College to be bolder, more distinctive and more globally engaged. It names India as a priority.

CLIFF is the College’s response. It supports the College’s commitments to social justice, sustainability, global citizenship and transformative education. It creates a structured platform for engagement with stakeholders in Cambridge, the UK, India and beyond.

The University has announced the Cambridge-India Centre for Advanced Studies. CLIFF is the first initiative at collegiate level in support of that commitment.

The first Indian academic to lead a Cambridge College was a Nobel laureate and Master of Trinity. CLIFF is an initiative of the first Indian practitioner to do so, the College’s 10th President.

“I am delighted to build on the strong and deep links between Cambridge University and India. The Cambridge-India CAS is an exciting opportunity to form collaborations with the best researchers and innovators in India and strengthen ties with such a rapidly growing knowledge economy.”

Professor Deborah Prentice, Vice-Chancellor, 27 January 2026

“By bringing together students, academics, alumni, and partners, CLIFF has the potential to make a significant contribution to the next chapter of the relationship between Cambridge and India.”

Professor Bhaskar Vira FAcSS, Pro-Vice-Chancellor for Education and Environmental Sustainability, in Harness, Issue 1
Design principles

How CLIFF works

Continuity

Build on the momentum, relationships and commitments already in place, including the Cambridge-India CAS.

Flexibility

Stay structured but not fixed. Adapt as new areas of research, collaboration and funding emerge.

Collaboration

Seek reciprocal partnerships across Cambridge, the UK and India, in government, civil society, education and industry.

Student centricity

Every activity ultimately serves students and alumni, whatever their nationality or discipline.

Impact

Connect academic work with practitioners, policymakers and civil society.

Programmes

Planned programmes

Student Futures

Scholarships, mentorship, leadership and entrepreneurship development, career conversations, and routes to internships.

Knowledge Exchange

A termly seminar series, visiting fellowships and joint research on governance, climate, AI policy, gender, urbanisation and public health.

Strategic Partnerships

Reciprocal collaborations with Indian institutions. Joint seminars, student dialogues and exchanges that move ideas and people both ways.

Public Dialogue

An annual CLIFF Lecture, India Futures Roundtables and student research showcases.

Cultural Understanding

Music, poetry, visual art and literary programming.

Why Lucy Cavendish

The College’s mission centres on access and inclusion. Its community is interdisciplinary, spanning law, policy, technology, public health, the arts and the social sciences.

Indian students are among the College’s largest overseas communities. Key members of its senior leadership are of Indian origin. It already holds partnerships with Indian educational institutions.

5NovThu
CLIFF Confluence series: Launch panel

Comprehensive Economic and Trade Agreement (CETA): Opportunities and Challenges

Thursday 5 November 20265:30 pm for a 5:45 pm startWood-Legh Room, Lucy Cavendish College

The UK and India signed CETA in July 2025. It came into force on 15 July 2026, less than three weeks after CLIFF itself was launched.

It is the UK’s most significant bilateral trade deal since Brexit. It is India’s most comprehensive agreement with a G7 economy. It sets a target of $100 billion in bilateral trade by 2030.

With the agreement in force, the focus moves to implementation. This panel launches CLIFF’s Confluence series. It brings together diplomatic, legal, business and investment views on what CETA means for both countries.

  • DiplomacyMs Nidhi Mani Tripathi IASMinister (Economic), High Commission of India, London
  • LawMr Abhijit MukhopadhyayPresident (Legal) and General Counsel, Hinduja Group
  • BusinessMr Sam TullyVisiting Fellow, Centre for India and Global Business, Judge Business School
  • InvestmentMr Pratik DattaniManaging Director, EPG Consulting, and Founder, Bridge India
  • ChairMr Brishni MukhopadhyayHead of Sustainable Investing, Western Asset Management

CLIFF Seminar series

Law, Sport and Social Justice: Lessons in Cricket from India, South Africa, and the UK

Friday 27 November 20265:30 pm for a 5:45 pm startWolfson Room, Lucy Cavendish College

This seminar draws on fieldwork in India and expert consultations in South Africa and the UK.

It examines how socio-economic, identity-based and systemic factors shape access to the sport, and what legal reform can and cannot change.

The seminar closes CLIFF’s Michaelmas Term programme.

CLIFF Seminar series

From Snow to Floods: Understanding Water in the Himalayas

Thursday 22 October 2026Wood-Legh Room, Lucy Cavendish College

The first event of CLIFF’s first full term came in the wake of recent floods in Nepal.

Dr Siddharth Gumber, Postdoctoral Researcher and Mountain Climate Modeller at the British Antarctic Survey, spoke on predicting the Himalayan snowfall that leads to floods. The talk combined field observations from India with modelling expertise from Cambridge and the UK.

26JunFri
The launch

Launch of the Cambridge Lucy India Futures Forum

Friday 26 June 2026Lucy Cavendish College

Dr Poorna Mysoor, Vice President of the College and Chair of CLIFF, lit the lamp. College President Girish Menon welcomed guests from across the University and beyond.

Bruno Cotta, Vice Chair, set out why India matters to Cambridge now. Student volunteers Aditya Prasanna Bhattacharya and Aaryani Dogra presented CLIFF’s design principles and planned activities. Jo Ryan, Development Director, explained how the College means to act on them.

Professor Jaideep Prabhu, Jawaharlal Nehru Professor of Indian Business and Enterprise at the Judge Business School, spoke on fitting CLIFF into the India-facing work already under way across the University. Jeeves Rohilla, President (PG) of the Students’ Union, made the case for student centricity. Shiraz Vira shared lessons from running exchange programmes between the UK and India. Professor Nirmala Rao OBE, Vice Chancellor of Krea University, spoke on the lead Lucy Cavendish had taken.

The afternoon closed with a reading from Nehru’s The Discovery of India, a recital of Tagore’s Shobujer Obhijaan, and a performance by the Cambridge University Indian Classical Music Society.

Guests included students from Trinity, Sidney Sussex, Christ’s, St Edmund’s and Murray Edwards, and from the Judge Business School, the Faculty of Law, and the Departments of Engineering, Mathematics and Physics.
